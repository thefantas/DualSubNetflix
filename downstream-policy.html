Privacy Policy — Downstream

Last updated: September 18, 2026

Downstream is a browser extension that finds the video a page is playing and saves it as a file. It has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking, it has no server of its own, and it does not sell or share data.

What stays on your device

WhatWhere it is keptUntil when
The list of media found on a tab: the media address, the page title and thumbnail, and the headers the page itself sent for that request — which is what lets the file be fetched again. The browser's session storage for the extension. Deleted when the tab navigates away or closes, and the whole store is gone when the browser quits.
Partly-assembled downloads, and the few seconds of video used for a card's moving preview. The browser's private filesystem (OPFS), readable by no website. Deleted once the finished file has been saved.
Your settings: interface language, preferred quality and container, whether previews are shown, download folder. The extension's own local storage. Until you change them or remove the extension.

What is sent over the network, and to whom

Only the site you are already on. The extension reads a playlist to see what is in it, and fetches the media itself when you ask it to download. Those requests go to the same servers the page was already using, and carry the headers the page itself sent. Nothing is sent anywhere else.

There is no server belonging to this extension, so there is nowhere for your data to be sent even in principle. Nothing about you, about the pages you visit or about what you download leaves your computer.

Permissions, and why each one exists

PermissionWhy
Access to all sites Video lives on any site, and its pieces routinely come from a CDN on a different domain than the page. A narrower list would work on no site at all. Pages are only observed; no script of the extension's runs on the pages you visit, with one exception noted below.
webRequest To notice that a page fetched a playlist or a media file. Read-only: Manifest V3 removed the ability to alter requests, so nothing is or can be changed in flight.
declarativeNetRequest A download must carry the same Cookie, Referer and Origin the page sent, or the CDN refuses it — and a normal fetch() is not allowed to set them. Rules are added for the one address about to be fetched and removed immediately afterwards.
downloads, storage, unlimitedStorage To save the finished file, remember your settings, and hold a large download while it assembles.
offscreen (Chrome only) A Manifest V3 service worker cannot start a Worker, and joining the pieces of a video into one file has to happen off the main thread. This permission opens a hidden document, belonging to the extension, that holds that Worker. It loads no page of yours and reaches no site. Firefox needs no equivalent and its build does not ask for it.
tabs, scripting To know which tab the panel is about, and to read that page's title and poster when you open the panel. The recording feature installs hooks on the single tab being recorded, only while it is recording.
notifications To say when a download has finished or failed.

The one exception to "no script on the pages you visit" is YouTube, which has a script of its own because its player cannot be read any other way. It reports what the player already knows about the video to the extension, and nowhere else.

Protected video

Video protected by DRM — Widevine, PlayReady, FairPlay — is detected and refused. The extension has no means of decrypting it and does not attempt to. Services such as Netflix, Disney+ and Prime Video are reported as protected and left alone.

Remote code

There is none. Everything the extension runs is included in the package you install, built from source before submission. Nothing is fetched and executed at runtime.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Any change that altered what is collected — there being nothing collected today — would be described here before it took effect.
